## Change-threshold decision

### Verified problem or evidence addressed

- The verified problem is not missing product scope; it is missing customer evidence.
- No real professor has accepted or rejected the invitation, completed the workflow, reported a genuine complaint, approved a revision, returned for a comparable second run, or made a payment-oriented commitment.
- Current competitors and institutional substitutes make extra generic generation, course-building, AI access, and integrations less—not more—defensible without evidence.

### Why this is the highest-leverage action now

Pausing product changes protects the validity of the next experiment. A real unaided run can expose where the product actually fails; changing the interface, workflow, price, privacy posture, or technical architecture first would add variables without resolving the decision.

### Expected customer or business outcome and metric

- Primary next evidence metric: one eligible professor acceptance or rejection with a reason.
- If accepted: one complete, real, schema-1.1 two-session record.
- Cohort target remains five complete real pilots.
- Decision-complete pilot records / completed pilots: target 100%; current 0/0.
- Payment-oriented commitments: target at least 2/5 before continuing the subscription thesis; current 0.
- No usefulness, time-saving, conversion, or retention improvement is claimed from today's decision.

### Why pause is preferable to no decision

An explicit pause prevents daily feature churn and defines what evidence reopens product work. A silent "no change" would leave future runs free to reinterpret the same market evidence as permission to build.

### Strongest practical alternatives considered

1. **Build professor accounts, billing, durable storage, or analytics.** These are sellable-beta requirements, but authentication, billing, retention, privacy, permissions, and sensitive-data behavior require approval. More importantly, they do not resolve the zero-pilot value and payer uncertainty.
2. **Add another assignment or discipline template.** Current evidence says needs vary by task and discipline, but there is no Workflow Lab failure showing which template is missing or worth maintaining.
3. **Add external AI revision.** The core direction ultimately requires AI-drafted corrections, but the current deterministic correction path is sufficient to test the human-approval and second-run loop. Adding provider behavior now increases cost and privacy risk without customer evidence.
4. **Change pricing or payer positioning.** Competitor prices and grants are anchors, not willingness-to-pay evidence. Pricing-model or major positioning changes require approval.
5. **Create another outreach or pilot artifact.** Project M and the product already contain a participant profile, invitation, protocol, schema, template, checker, evaluator, and decision rules. Another artifact would be redundant.

### Validation needed to decide whether the pause worked

- A real eligible professor accepts or rejects the existing invitation.
- If accepted, the first run is completed without coaching and recorded in schema 1.1.
- Product work resumes immediately only for a verified critical safety or completion defect.
- Otherwise, preserve the tested product through the comparable second run and ask willingness-to-pay questions afterward.
- Apply the precommitted cohort evaluator after five complete real records; do not adjust thresholds after seeing outcomes.
